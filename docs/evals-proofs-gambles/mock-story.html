Chapter 2 · Proofs
Explanatory proofs

Which proof explains it best?

There are many ways to prove the same fact. Thirty-eight mathematicians compared nine proofs of one theorem, two at a time, and picked the one that better explains why it is true. Their 760 choices give every proof a score.

I gave four AI models the same pairs, each pair in both orders and ten times over, and fitted the same scores to their choices.

⌄ What am I looking at?

Picking the shorter proof matches the mathematicians about as well as the best model (r 0.85). Remove length and the rule falls to 0.09, while the models keep 0.62–0.78.

So the models are not just counting lines. They still disagree with mathematicians on specific proofs: they like the Taylor series proof more and the proof by contradiction less.

Chapter 3 · Gambles
Risky choices

Would you take this bet?

In the choices13k study, thousands of people chose between two gambles. People are not pure calculators: they often take the safer bet even when the other one pays more on average. I took 101 of these problems and asked the same four models two things.

Problem 225 · 16 people answered
Which would most people pick?

66% of people chose A. Asked to predict, the models said 22%–37% would choose B.
PlayThe model makes the choice itself, ten times per problem.
PredictThe model guesses what share of people chose B.

Asked to play, the models mostly pick the bet that pays more on average. Asked to predict people, they side with the majority more often.
